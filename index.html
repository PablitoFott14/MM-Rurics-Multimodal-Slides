Task Authoring
Intro course · Contributor level
OpenClaw agents work inside a persona's own apps and data: their inbox, their files, their calendar, their photos. This course is about building the tasks that put those agents under real pressure: a scenario with something at stake, media the answer genuinely depends on, a conversation that unfolds over several turns, and the rubrics that decide whether the model got it right.

Section 1

Your Role

You are not answering tasks. You are building the thing that grades a model.
  • You design 2 to 5 turn multimodal tasks that stress tools, media and reasoning together
  • You then write the rubric set that defines what a correct answer had to look like
  • Every task ships with a golden. Proof the task is solvable at all

What you produce

  • A universe grounded scenario and Story Draft
  • One opening prompt + up to four reactive follow ups
  • Objective and subjective rubric blocks

Mindset

  • Do not optimise for first try success
  • A task the model breezes through carries no signal
  • Difficulty must come from the work, not from trivia
Your rubrics become ground truth in real model evaluation. A criterion that grades something the prompt never asked for does not just lose you a task. It teaches the model the wrong thing.

The Hard Rules

Parameters that are checked before anything else is read
  • Multi turn only. 2 to 5 total turns. The opening prompt is auto sent; you add at most four follow ups
  • Universe adherence. Solvable with the Universe Explorer and Input Files, and nothing else
  • Required identifiers. Environment ID and Service Universe Artifact ID, added in the app
  • Realism. The scenario has to plausibly fit the persona, the category and the subcategory
A seeded user simulator replays your follow up intent on later runs, including the Golden Trajectory. Follow ups must remain reactive in wording and stable in intent.
Do not deliver tasks with trajectories that do not represent actual failures or that force failure just to reach the 50% threshold.
Turn budget
Model A is capped.
Model B is not.
Model A: opening prompt + up to four follow ups.
Model B: as many intent level hints as it takes to reach the golden. The cap belongs to the graded leg, not to the demonstration leg.

The Three Required Stages

Acquire → Process → Decide → Output
  • Acquire. Gather evidence from tools, files and media
  • Process. Reconcile, rank, score, calculate, apply rules
  • Output. Produce a defined, verifiable artifact
The stages have to be dependent. If the output could be written without the processing step, or the processing could run without the acquisition step, you have three activities rather than three stages.
Multimodal requirement: at least one core requirement must depend on examining visual, audio or other media. The intended dimension level depends on how many artifacts must be examined.
Worked shape
Acquire
Pull the vendor list out of the shutdown channel; open the receipt photos attached to the prompt.
Process
Pair each channel claim with the evidence that supports it. Three groups fall out: cancelled, unconfirmed, not cancelled.
Decide
A vendor with a message but no receipt is not cancelled. That call is the task.
Output
The three groups and the amount owed, written into the named artifact.

Section 2

Mandatory Architecture

If a short single turn linear interaction could finish it, it is too simple

✓  Every task must have

  • Multi stage coordination with real dependencies between stages
  • Real decision logic. Ranking, thresholds, rule based comparison, conditionals
  • Multi system coordination that text only reasoning cannot replace
  • Realistic friction. Ambiguity, conflicting sources, missing fields, imperfect media
  • Multi turn depth. A real user genuinely needs to come back
  • A defined artifact, not exploratory conversation
  • Model differentiation. Room for models to perform differently

✗  Do not submit

  • Single turn or single step workflows, plain summarisation, basic search
  • Static reasoning with no tool coordination
  • Tasks impossible with the universe loadout
  • Tasks likely to score identically across models
  • Decorative multimodal input nobody has to inspect
  • Image-to-CSV transcription on its own, which is trivial integration
  • Perfectly clean, curated inputs
Length is not depth. A task that touches four servers across seven steps but draws every fact from one source is a long chore. Overloading a prompt with unrelated requirements is a listed prompt defect, not a difficulty lever.

Section 4

Start With The Universe

Not with the prompt. The loadout decides what the task can be.
  • Add the Service Universe Artifact ID before you draft a single line
  • Open the Universe Explorer and confirm which servers and data sources are actually loaded
  • Interact with the AI agent early. Ask it about available tools, data, constraints and workflows worth testing
  • Use SQL queries to inspect tables, relationships and edge cases. Ask the AI agent to help write or refine a query when useful
  • Let actual depth shape the next action. Follow what you uncover instead of a rigid sequence
  • Keep a multimodal perspective. Combine universe findings and multimodal inputs into one realistic, complex context
  • Confirm there is enough material to sustain the opening prompt and its follow ups
Designing for Gmail when only messaging is loaded is an environment defect, not a model failure. The task will be rejected.
“Undocumented” does not mean “infeasible”. Test the workflow with the AI agent, SQL, the CLI and the tool surface before ruling it out.
Recon checklist
☐  Service Universe Artifact ID added
☐  Every required server and data source is loaded
☐  AI agent asked to surface tools, data and feasible workflows
☐  SQL used to inspect records and relationships
☐  Static dates and canonical identifiers checked
☐  Later steps depend on evidence or state found earlier
☐  Feasibility confirmed before drafting

Section 4

Check It, Never Assume It

Static dates and unverified capabilities are the two quiet ways a task breaks

⚠  Dates are static

  • Universe calendars hold fixed dates and do not move with the day the task is run, so “next Tuesday” can land on a week that holds nothing
  • Anchor to a range you have actually seen in the Universe Explorer, and write that date reference into the prompt: “the week of 12 May”, not “next Tuesday”
  • Pinned in the prompt, the temporal context stays static and unambiguous. Model A, Model B and the reviewer all read the same window
BAD   Can you check what I have on next Tuesday?
GOOD  Can you check what I have on Tuesday 12 May?

?  Not sure it is possible

  • Ask the AI agent directly. It will tell you what it can reach and what it cannot, faster than guessing from the outside
  • Open Agent tool access. It lists the tools each server exposes, so you can read the real surface before ruling a workflow out
  • A server with few tools is not a weak server. Check what it covers first
🔧  Agent tool access every tool, server by server
In the chat panel, /slack /email /calendar /fintrack /messaging generate the SQL for you
Every fact the model needs must be reachable in the prompts, the input files or the universe data. If it is reachable nowhere, the task is unsolvable and no rubric written against it can be defended.

Section 4

Universe And Multimodal Context Together

Each has to hold part of the answer, and neither half should stand alone

✓  What that looks like

  • A figure read off a photo is what lets the model find the matching record in the universe
  • The universe supplies what the media cannot: earlier messages, contacts, transaction history, the state of a folder
  • The model has to read the universe, not only write to it. Two or three servers coordinate toward one goal
The handwritten total on a photographed delivery sheet is the only place that figure appears. The payment it should match sits in the finance server, and the two do not agree.

✗  Four ways it goes wrong

  • Write only. Every figure comes from the attachments and the universe is only ever written to. Notes, calendar entries and a draft are output, not interaction
  • Universe dropped. The media settles everything and nothing is reconciled against what the universe holds
  • Pictures of the universe. Not simply an image that relates to universe evidence. The defect is an input with no natural reason to exist, or one that copies what the universe already holds while the task pretends it does not: reconciling a receipt screenshot whose amount already sits in FinTrack, email or the messaging thread
  • A cross check nobody could perform. The reconciliation hangs on an input no person could read, or one contrived past plausibility. Nothing there can be rated, and the model is failed for a defect in the task
An image has to be the only or most reliable source of at least one fact the task needs. A screenshot of the workspace never is.
The test is ablation. Take the attachments away and ask whether the task still lands. Performance has to fall by at least 0.50 against the full run, so if the answer barely moves, the media is decorative and the task gets rejected.

Idea Quality
Section 5

The Story Draft

The planning document you write before anything runs
It holds four things: the Agent Objective, the Desired Outcome, the category and subcategory pair, and the input_files/ ZIP covered on the next slide. The Objective says why the task exists and for whom. The Outcome says what the finished answer has to look like.

Agent Objective

The persona, the problem, the stakes and what success looks like. Never the implementation.
  • Captures a real persona with real stakes, why the task matters to them, the artifact types success implies, constraints stated as outcomes
  • Never “act as X agent” role play, tool by tool steps, a numbered pipeline, or policy thresholds lifted out of the input files

Desired Outcome

The inspectable end state. The shape of the answer, not its content.
  • Captures required artifact names and structure, column names, enums, decision logic, conditional branches, and how the result will be verified
  • Never a verbatim answer key, tool fetched values such as balances or names, or an unanchored “good quality output”
GOOD  Jordan owns an online running shoe store and needs to triage five
      customer complaints with photos before replying today.
BAD   Act as the escalation agent; use the messaging server, the finance
      server and the uploaded images to...
None of this is embedded in the platform and the agent never reads it, so the opening prompt is the only channel to the model. Any requirement a rubric enforces has to appear in the prompt or a file the prompt points at. A heading that lives only in the Desired Outcome cannot be graded.

Hard rules, and each one is checked

DownsamplingVideo to 480p. Images to 1080p or lower. Audio to MP3 at 64 kbps.
Audio lengthKeep it short. Past roughly 5 to 10 minutes risks a timeout or prompt error.
FormatsNever generate .xlsx, .docx or .pdf with an LLM. No HEIC either: a file that crashes the run is your mistake, not the model's.
LLM generated capNo more than 20% of all artifacts.
No answer leaksFilenames, manifests and helper documents must not reveal the finding.
No junk filesNo .env, no .key, no leftovers, no meaningless names.
CopyrightCC0 and CC-BY are fine. Avoid CC-BY-NC, Unsplash, Getty and paywalled sources.
PIINo faces of non public figures. Synthetic persona data only.
Messy is not the same as unusable. Blurry but readable is realism; illegible to a human is a task defect you will not be able to grade against.

The Opening Prompt

One turn, sent verbatim, in the user's voice
The prompt is not created at this step. It belongs to scenario design, not to Prompt Generation, and is drafted in the Section 3 walkthrough. Define it only here and you will be back adjusting the Story Draft, the input files and the follow ups to keep everything aligned.

✓  Natural

“I uploaded the results of the analysis.”
“I took photos too but honestly the video is
the clearest thing I have, especially for
the bathroom.”
The user points at what matters the way a person would. Finding the fact inside it is the model's job.

✗  Over explicit

“I uploaded IMG_124234.png, IMG_2342384.jpg
and IMG_1234.jpg, where you can find the
results of the analysis.”
“You can find the receipt in
Screenshot 23-05-2026 24234324.jpg”  # it is not in it
The first removes the search. The second is worse. It traps the model with content that is not there.

Naming the deliverables

BAD   “generate a report with your findings”
BAD   “create an HTML page to materialize this idea”
GOOD  “write your findings in final_report.md”
GOOD  “materialize this idea in landing_page.html”
Every file a rubric grades is named in the prompt body with unambiguous create or update intent, including files introduced in a later turn.
Section 6 · Prompt Generation
Prompt Rules
Section 6

The Follow Up Turns

Decide the intent before you run. Write the words after you have read the answer.
TURN 1
The opening prompt, sent automatically
TURNS 2 TO 5
One to four follow ups, written by you
A task with no follow up is not a multi turn task. Two turns is the floor, five the ceiling.

✓  Four levers, in the user's own voice

  • A deferred asset. The thing the user said they would dig out
    “Here is the photo of the delivery slip. The handwritten total is the one to trust.”
  • A clarification held back. Something they knew in turn 1 and did not think to say
    “Forgot to mention, Marta covered the Tuesday shift, so that one is not mine.”
  • A revision to the artifact. The audit checks that the model edits it rather than rebuilding it
    “Split the summary into one row per invoice. Leave the rest as it is.”
  • A cross check against another source. The one that usually finds the failure
    “Before I send this, does it line up with what FinTrack has?”

✗  What breaks a follow up

  • Hinting. “You missed the total on the receipt” turns leg 1 into leg 2. You lose the failure you were about to grade, and the audit checks whether the simulator leaked ground truth
  • Wording fixed in advance. A turn written before you read the one it answers will contradict what the model actually did, and it reads as a script rather than a person
  • A new topic. Follow ups build on the artifact. An unrelated ask is asking for the sake of asking, and it is a listed defect
  • Nothing left to do. If turn 1 resolved everything, the follow up becomes a thank you note. That is a turn 1 problem
  • Patching your own gap. A file you forgot, a rule you left out. Fix the task and run it again instead of repairing it mid conversation
Everything you ask for in a follow up becomes a milestone and something the rubrics have to grade. Three requests in one turn is three requirements, not one.

Section 7

The Two Legs

Same prompt, two runs, two different jobs
  • Leg 1 · Model A. Produces the trajectory objective rubrics are authored and rated against
  • Milestones. Convert that conversation into intent level guidance
  • Leg 2 · Model B. Same opening prompt verbatim, hinted through the milestones until the golden is reached
Objective rubrics grade Model A. Subjective rubrics are created by comparing Model A and Model B, the golden, while stepping into the user’s shoes. They are not based on any specific trajectory, but on what, according to the scenario intent, meaningfully improves or harms the user experience.
There is no separate environment and no “silver trajectory”. Everything runs through the Task Runner on the same platform.
Four things people get wrong
✗ “Model B is capped at four follow ups too.”
It is not. Hint as long as it takes.
✗ “Write the subjective block before Model B runs.”
It is written after the golden exists.
✗ “Subjective rubrics are rated on Model B alone.”
Both artifacts, side by side.
✗ “A milestone may name the target value so B can be steered.”
Never. That is answer leaking.

Leg 1 · Model A

Run it, capture it, then prove it was hard enough

Running the leg

  • Let the opening prompt post automatically
  • Download every turn's output from app/outputs/ before you reply
  • Write each follow up reactively, against what the model actually produced
  • Finish with Complete Conversation, then Extract Trajectories. Never force the session to end
  • Author the objective block after considering the whole conversation, not just the last turn

The 50% failure gate

  • List every genuine model mistake across the conversation
  • Estimate the rubric weight each one would fail
  • Model A must fail at least 50% of total rubric weight for genuine reasons
  • Below the bar? Add cross modal reconciliation, implicit cross checks or conditional logic

Section 7 · Task Runner
Model A
Section 8

Valid vs Invalid Failures

Telling these apart is the single most tested judgement on this project

✓  Valid: a decision the model made

  • Misread a legible value. A figure any person could read off the file came through wrong, and the error carried into the artifact
  • Filename taken as evidence. A session called completed without the file being opened. Still valid even if the file would have supported the conclusion, because the claim was never grounded
  • Universe evidence never found. The record was there and reachable, and the model answered without it
  • Cross reference collapsed to one side. Asked to reconcile the media against the universe, it trusted one of them and reported that number as the answer
  • Logic flow misapplied. The prompt and the multimodal context each carry part of the rule, and the model dropped a step or applied them in the wrong order
  • An accessible tool left unused. The server is loaded and the model estimates instead of querying it
  • Conditional handled wrong. The plan marks a session optional and it is counted anyway, or a branch trips that the ground truth never trips and an artifact appears that nobody asked for
  • Fabricated precision. Measurements stated to the millimetre with no ruler in frame

✗  Not valid: your defect, or the platform's

  • prompt-error, idle timeout, session abort, trajectory execution error. The run broke, the model never got to decide anything
  • A HEIC file that crashes or times out the run. A forbidden input format is your defect, not a weakness you found
  • A rubric demanding a heading that appears only in the Desired Outcome
  • A photo no human could read either
  • A source conflict where the prompt never said which source wins, so either answer was defensible
  • Contradictory multimodal input. The prompt asks for the file to be used one way, and the file itself carries an instruction or evidence that rewrites the task
  • Contrived scenario. Verbosity and extra asks piled on to make the task look harder, with no realistic background behind them
  • Data that is not in the server, asked for anyway, then the model penalised for not finding it
Never turn an invalid failure into failing rubrics, and do not count it as evidence the task is hard. Fix the task instead.

Failure Judgement
Section 9

Anatomy of a Criterion

Binary statements about whether Model A met a requirement

Scope

  • Written and rated against Model A only, across the entire conversation: the opening prompt and every follow up
  • Emphasise where Model A failed. That is where the grading signal is
  • Never leave a later turn ungraded because the early turns were more interesting

Polarity

WeightNot PresentPresent
+1  +3  +5FailureSuccess
−1  −3  −5SuccessFailure
Either failing cell, positive and Not Present or negative and Present, requires a three part justification. No exceptions.

Six properties every criterion carries

Outcome based. Grades deliverables and state changes, not whether a tool was reached for
Positively phrased. Says what the model should have done; the signed weight carries polarity
Self contained. Ratable from the response or trajectory alone, without reopening the inputs
Prompt aligned. Every literal filename, heading or value appears in the prompt or a file it points at
Atomic. One evaluable outcome per criterion
Intent focused. Grades whether the request was satisfied, not wording the prompt never required

Self contained

✗ Response identifies the first president
  of the USA.
✓ Response identifies the first president
  of the USA as George Washington.

✗ xyz.csv includes the first row from abc.png.
✓ xyz.csv includes the first non-header row
  "John Smith", "123-456-7890", "$300".

Atomic: read the finding, not the punctuation

✓ The model identifies that UT_part2.py
  returns failing results and concludes bug
  NEXB-2345 is not resolved.  # one finding
✗ ...identifies NEXB-2345 as not resolved and
  NEXB-2401 as resolved.  # can diverge
✗ ...identifies NEXB-2345 as not resolved and
  records it in the report.  # two targets
An “and”, a semicolon or several clauses are not defects while every clause serves the same finding.

The four difficulty axes

Tool / source coordination. Three or more sources reconciled, or tools chained
Reasoning depth. Multi step inference, conditionals, calculation
Modality. Cross modal work across image, data and text
Discovery effort. Hidden fields, cross document tracing
“The model identifies Helpshift as cancelled by reconciling the #winddown message at a given timestamp with a screenshot confirming a final amount due of $1,500.00.”
1  Coordination. The channel message plus the attached screenshot
3  Discovery. Nobody linked the two; the model has to find the pairing
2  Modality. The amount exists only inside the image
4  Reasoning. An intention to cancel is not a cancellation until evidence confirms it
Agent Behavior · Trajectory · +5. Contrast: “audit_report.md exists” is mechanical → +1. Pulling one value out of an image and reporting it is a single cross modal step → +3.
Not Tool Use, which is reserved for cases where the user’s intent genuinely requires a specific tool, and not Final Answer Artifact either, since nothing here is being checked inside a file. Only −5 −3 −1 +1 +3 +5 are valid weights. There is no +2 and no +4.

Section 9

Building the Block

Composition, the recurring rejections, and the justification every failure needs

Composition

  • Cover every requirement positively first, then add negatives: around 25% of the block, hard ceiling 30%
  • A negative asserts the model was tempted into something specific. If you cannot point at the temptation in the run or in the design, it does not belong in the block
  • One repeated mistake is not 20 rubrics. Write a volume criterion, “the agent sends all 16 required emails”, plus up to about five deterministic spot checks
  • Stacked OR logic only where the outcomes are genuinely equivalent, never to patch thin coverage

✗  Rejected

“MEMORY.md lists coderabbit under UNCONFIRMED and does not list Datadog anywhere in the file.”
Two assertions that pass or fail independently. One rating can no longer express the result.
“The model does not fail to consult the #winddown channel before classifying any vendor.”
Double negative. State what the model should have done and let the weight carry the polarity.
“The final response is written in a warm, professional register.”
A bare adjective with no observable anchor. Register belongs in the subjective block, tied to something a reviewer can point at.

The three parts of a failure justification

Every failing rubric on Model A carries one: a positive criterion rated Not Present, or a negative criterion rated Present. No exceptions.
1 · Why the rubric is correct. Quote the prompt or the input policy that grounds it
2 · Where Model A failed. Cite the turn, tool use or output holding the mistake
3 · Why it matters. The impact on a correct and complete answer
Definitive, never speculative, and referenced to the prompt or the trajectory directly. Never cite other models or pass rates. If you cannot write all three, delete the rubric. The golden needs no justifications.
Other recurring defects: prompt and rubric grading different deliverables · over specifying wording the prompt never required · hard coded oracle values · contradictory polarity pairs · a visual check spanning several files · an input perception criterion nobody can evaluate.

Milestones

The turn by turn intent spec you drive the second leg from
In the finished pipeline a user simulator plays every turn after the first, reproducing your intent across runs that diverge. Until that is wired up, you are the simulator, and these milestones are the only script you have.
FieldWhat goes in it
TurnWhich turn of the Model A conversation the requirement came from
ModifierWhat this turn does to work that already exists. Covered on the next slide
IntentWhat the user wants at this point, stripped of anything specific to the response you happened to receive
Follow up promptHow to express that intent to a run that produced something different
Continuation criteriaWhat must be true in the agent’s output before you move on to the next milestone
Assets delivered hereAny files handed over at this turn, if the task defers assets

One per requirement, grouped by turn

  • A follow up carrying three requirements produces three milestones, not one
  • Every milestone is attributable to an identifiable turn
  • No filenames and no values. “The progress is kept in the created HTML file” is the register expected

Intent, never the answer

INTENT  The information from the test image is added
        to the video.
ANSWER  Add the $126 price to the video.
INTENT  The new photographs are taken into the existing
        room by room assessment.
ANSWER  The bathroom moves to finished.
The test: if a milestone only makes sense against the wording the first model produced, rewrite it so it still lands on a run that took another path.

Milestones
Section 10

Milestone Modifiers

Every milestone carries one. It records what the turn does to work that already exists, which is what makes the turn worth having.
ModifierWhen to use itWhat you do as the user
openingThe first turn, and any later turn that simply adds a new requirement without touching earlier workSend the opening prompt verbatim. On a later opening turn, state the new requirement and nothing else
deferred_assetMaterial the opening turn did not put in scope arrives, or is released into scope, at this turnHand it over, or say it is now in scope, and require it to be integrated against work already done. Never let the agent start over
clarification_gatedThe opening prompt is genuinely underspecified on one decision the agent cannot correctly guessHold the answer until the agent asks. Do not volunteer it. If it never asks and proceeds anyway, that is a model failure: record it, do not rescue it
revisionAn artifact has been delivered and the brief now changesChange one thing, precisely. Correct behaviour is a targeted edit, not a rebuild. Keep the change small enough that regenerating is visibly wasteful

How to choose

  • Turn 1 is opening unless the task withholds something from scope, in which case it is deferred_asset
  • A turn that adds a genuinely new requirement without touching earlier work stays opening, however late it falls
  • A turn that changes something already delivered is revision, not opening

Driving the leg from them

  • Work the milestones in order. Do not skip ahead because the agent got somewhere early. Check the continuation criteria first
  • Express the same intent the same way every time. Variation between runs must come from the model, not from you
  • Steer toward the milestone. Never hand over its answer
The same three appear again as task level modifiers, with a target mix across the whole bank: deferred assets on 40% of tasks, clarification gated on 30%, revision on 30%. A task may carry several. Any modifier assigned to your task has to genuinely show up in the conversation, not just in the form.

Section 10

Milestones · Worked

A renovation audit: the scope, the contractor's tracker, a walk through video

✓  Respects the divisions the turn has

Turn 1 makes four separate asks, so they are spread across separate checks:
  • Each room's standing against what was agreed, on the range the user asked for
  • Which agreed items inside each room are finished and which are not
  • What the contractor reports set against what is actually there
  • Work appearing that the signed scope does not authorise

✗  Two ways to lose the part

Everything folded into one milestone. Four asks that do not collapse into each other, stated inside a single check, is a paraphrase of the prompt rather than an intent spec.
The wrong standard. “Each room is rated after comparing what the tracker says against what the agreement says” rates progress against the contractor's claim, not against the scope, and it never compares the claim to what the video shows.
Where the divisions are genuinely arguable, either reading is fine. Whether a later turn's update and its grounding are one requirement or two is a judgement call. The line to hold is between stated and implied. A requirement spelled out earns its place wherever it sits; one the reviewer has to infer does not.
A constraint the user states, such as the rating range, is not a leak. Requiring each room to be placed on it is intent. What leaks is the position a room ends up at.

Section 11

Leg 2 · The Golden Run

Same prompt, same universe, a different model, and no cap on turns
The golden is the ideal response: what the Model A conversation should have arrived at. You are the user simulator again, and the whole job of this leg is to drive a model there.

What changes from leg 1

  • The same eight step workflow, the same opening prompt sent verbatim, the same universe
  • A different model runs it. If leg 1 went against GPT, this one goes against Opus
  • No turn limit. The two to five turn tier applied to the authoring leg only
  • Attention nudges and a cleanup turn are fine here, as long as they stay at intent level

The steering loop

  • It stops at prompt agent with your opening prompt already loaded. Wait for the first response
  • Read that response against your current milestone, not against the run you saw in leg 1
  • Milestone met, move to the next one. Not met, hint again and read again
  • When the last milestone is satisfied, complete the conversation and the remaining steps finish on their own

What a hint looks like

HINT  I still cannot see anything from the test
      image reflected in the video.
LEAK  Add the $126 price to the video.
HINT  The bathroom photos I sent should be part
      of the room by room view by now.
LEAK  Mark the bathroom as finished.
A hint says what is still missing from the user’s point of view. It never says what to write. If the model can copy your hint into the artifact, you handed over the answer.
However many hints it takes, every one of them stays at intent level. “Add $126” ends the value of the leg. Steer toward the milestone, never hand over its answer, and keep the intent identical to the one you used in leg 1. Variation between the two runs has to come from the model.
Section 11 · Model B and Golden
The Golden Run
Section 11

What Makes It Golden

The bar the artifact has to clear, and the three gates on the way out

The artifact

  • The same deliverable in its best form, not a different or a larger deliverable
  • Satisfies every objective requirement, with the filenames the prompt named
  • Every factual claim grounded in the actual inputs: images opened, videos watched, PDFs read. Nothing asserted from a filename
  • Reachable with the universe loadout alone, and it reconciles the media against at least one other source rather than reporting it

Three gates on the way out

  • Prove it. You cannot move past this step until the scenario has been shown to be achievable, by achieving it
  • Download the trajectory and the collected artifacts. Both renders are what the subjective block gets written from. Skipping it here costs you the next section
  • Trajectory Validator. It runs on the way out, the same as the rubric one. If it scores you three or below and you disagree, select No and justify it

What the golden is not

Not a bigger deliverable. Sections nobody asked for make it a different task, not a better answer
Not a rewritten prompt. If getting there needed the prompt changed, the prompt is what is wrong
Not a lucky run. Reaching it once by accident proves nothing. You have to be able to steer it there

When the golden will not come

Hinting forever is the wrong response. If the ideal answer stays out of reach, the diagnosis is upstream and one of these is the reason:
The universe does not carry it. The task needs a skill or a table the loadout never had. The idea is what has to change
The milestone was written against one run. It only makes sense to a model that took leg 1’s route. Rewrite it at intent level
The prompt never named the deliverable. You are hinting your way around a gap the opening prompt should have closed
Nothing in the inputs settles it. If no source answers the question, no model can, and the rubric grading it would be ungradable too
This leg is also what makes the 50% failure gate coherent rather than contradictory. Model A has to miss at least half its criteria while the same task, the same prompt and the same universe come out right here. If you cannot reach the ideal response yourself, the scenario was never solvable and the thing to fix is the task, not the model.

Section 12

Subjective Rubrics

Presentation only, and the block where most of the failures have been landing
The dividing line is simple. Anything you can settle against a source of truth stays in the objective block. Anything you judge by looking at the render lives here.

The presentation scale

+5  Critically important. Unusable for its audience without it
−1  Slightly detrimental. A flaw that compromises nothing
+3  Important. Core presentation competence
−3  Detrimental. Materially degrades readability
+1  Slightly important. Polish
−5  Critically detrimental. Breaks it as a presented object
These are not the objective difficulty weights. Here the number says how much the property matters to the reader, not how hard it was to achieve. Negatives are expected: around 25% of the set, ceiling 30%.

Which block does it belong to?

CriterionWhyBlock
The report carries a section titled SummaryThe prompt asked for itObjective
Heading levels form a real hierarchy across the documentNobody asked. You see it in the renderSubjective
The total owed reads 4,032.00Settled against a source of truthObjective
The total is set larger than the entries beneath itJudged by looking at itSubjective
Every criterion names one observable property of the render, so two evaluators land on the same answer. Bare adjectives are the most common rejection here. The block stays at or below 30% of total task weight, never repeats a value check the objective block already covers, and only exists once the golden does.

Rating and Justifying

The only block rated against both runs, and the last thing you do on the task
 StepWhat it involves
1Rate against both runsPresent or Not Present on Model A and on Model B, criterion by criterion. The evaluation viewer lays the whole set out, both responses against every criterion. Keep both renders open, the same two you compared when you wrote them
2Check the pre filled sideOne side comes back pre filled from the autorater, and the auto generated criteria appear here too. Both are a starting point at most. Check that side rather than trust it, and do the other yourself
3Pairwise preferenceA fixed set of comparisons with both artifacts open: which is more usable by the requester, which handles visual presentation better, which better matches the brief as it stood at the end, and overall preference
4Justify, Model A onlyEvery positive that came out Not Present and every negative that came out Present. Same three parts as the objective block, pointed at the render rather than the trajectory

A failing justification, worked

“Each of the three groups is presented as a visually distinct section.”  Not Present on Model A.
1 · Why it is correct. The brief asked for the vendors grouped by status, and grouping that is not visible on the page is not grouping
2 · Where it failed. The render runs all sixteen entries as one continuous list with no separation between statuses
3 · Why it matters. The reader cannot tell at a glance which vendors are cancelled, the one question the artifact exists to answer
The recurring rejections here: a bare adjective with no anchor · a deterministic value check sitting in this block · criteria written before the golden existed · a block rated against one artifact only · a stylistic requirement the prompt actually stated, which belongs in the objective block.
Presentation judgement is the part of this task that is genuinely yours. The autorater cannot do it for you, and it is where most of the failures land.
